Welcome to the experiment
"Calc-Additions"

Thank you for considering participating in this experiment and supporting scientific research.

Many people struggle to remember numbers in their daily lives. This experiment attempts to study how people retain numbers in their working memory. It is the first time this question has been investigated using such a task.

The tasks in this experiment: Your task will involve performing mental calculations. The problems will consist of adding numbers from 11 to 19. To remain accurate, this requires attention over the flow of the experiment. We ask that you do not listen to any music or podcasts in-between because it will needlessly slow you down. These are simple tasks in which you must react as quickly and accurately as possible.

Conditions of participation:

  • Your mother tongue is English.
  • You must use a personal computer or a laptop with a keyboard.
  • You have not yet participated in the experiment.
  • You have a stable Internet connection.
  • You are in a quiet environment. Please do not listen to music or a podcast while completing this experiment.

Stopping the experiment: You can stop the experiment and close the window at any time, even if you previously agreed to participate, without any consequences. However, you will not have access to the participation funds. You have as much time as you need to make your decision.

Your payment: You will receive £6 (via Prolific) for your participation once the experiment is complete (scheduled for February). Payment information will be displayed at the end of the experiment.

Your data: The experiment will record the keys pressed on your computer and the time they were pressed. Once the experiment is complete, the experimental data will be stored separately from any personal information such as contact details and payment information. At the end of the experiment, you will be able to download all the data collected throughout the experiment and transferred to us.

Any questions? If you accept, you may change your mind at any time, without giving us a reason, by simply sending an email to roman.janssen@u-paris.fr or by mail to the following address:
